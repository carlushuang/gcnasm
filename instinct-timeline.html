AMD Instinct timeline: MI6 to MI455X

From the Radeon R9 Fury X, AMD's first HBM GPU (2015), through every AMD Instinct datacenter GPU to the CDNA 5 parts of 2026: by architecture and gfx target, with how memory, bandwidth and peak throughput grew.

19 parts · 2017–2026 GCN 3 → CDNA 5 gfx803 → gfx950 4 GB → 432 GB
In one paragraph

AMD's datacenter GPUs started as Radeon Instinct in June 2017 with three cards on three architectures: MI8 (Fiji), MI6 (Polaris) and MI25 (Vega). MI50 and MI60 (Vega 20, 7 nm) followed in November 2018. MI100 (November 2020) began CDNA, a compute-only line with matrix cores, under the AMD Instinct name. CDNA 2 put two dies in one package (MI250X); CDNA 3 moved to chiplets with a 256 MiB Infinity Cache and FP8 (MI300X, and the MI300A with Zen 4 cores); CDNA 4 added the MX formats and 160 KiB of LDS (MI355X); CDNA 5 moves to HBM4 and the Helios rack (MI455X, July 2026). Over nine years, memory per GPU grew from 4 GB to 432 GB, bandwidth from 0.224 to 23.3 TB/s, and dense FP16 from 5.7 TFLOPS to 5 PFLOPS.

About the names

There is no MI10. The first three names roughly track peak FP16 TFLOPS: MI6 5.7, MI8 8.2, MI25 24.6. From MI100 on, the number marks the series, and the letters mark the variant: X for the flagship OAM part, A for the APU, P for the PCIe card.

FP64 is 5 TFLOPS on MI455X and MI450X; MI430X is the FP64 part (288 TFLOPS projected, 2027).
